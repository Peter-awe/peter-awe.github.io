3The Correction Paradox

Children acquiring speech make systematic substitutions (e.g., /w/ for /r/). These are not errors to be corrected -- they carry developmental information. When ASR models are fine-tuned on children's speech, they learn to "correct" these patterns, destroying clinically relevant information.

Example: "rabbit" /r/ → /w/ substitution

The Correction Paradox: The adapted model maps the child's /w/ back to the adult /ɹ/. Word-level accuracy improves (PER drops from 25.19% to 24.93%), but the developmental substitution pattern -- which SLPs use for diagnosis -- is erased. Liquids show the largest damage: PER increases from 28.48% to 45.22% at the phoneme level.

6Evidence Chain

Our argument is built on a seven-step causal chain. Click each step to learn more.

1
Factorial ANOVA
Adaptation strategy significantly affects PER (F=32.23, p<.001)
+ details
A two-way factorial ANOVA (Strategy x Epoch) reveals a significant main effect of adaptation strategy on phoneme error rate (F=32.23, p<.001, Cohen's d=0.85). This confirms that how we adapt the model matters, not just how long we train it.
2
Training Dynamics
CPT degrades then recovers; freezing stabilizes early
+ details
Standard CPT shows a characteristic U-shaped curve: PER initially worsens as pre-trained representations are disrupted, then partially recovers as the model re-learns. Layer freezing bypasses this degradation entirely, achieving competitive PER (25.34%) from early epochs without the representation damage.
3
Layer Probing (Level A)
Age in early layers (L1=77.9%), rate in late layers (L24=81.4%)
+ details
Probing classifiers trained on frozen layer representations show that age-group information concentrates in early layers (Layer 1: 77.9%) while speaking-rate peaks in late layers (Layer 24: 81.4%). Paired t-test confirms significant separation (t=3.36, p=.028). This is Level A evidence: the information is decodable.
4
Concept Erasure (Level B)
Removing age causes +13.73% PER increase
+ details
Iterative Null-space Projection (INLP) removes age information from the representations. The resulting +13.73% PER increase is Level B causal evidence: the model is not merely encoding age -- it actively relies on age representations for phoneme recognition. This moves beyond correlation (probing) to intervention.
5
Layer Freezing
Freeze-6 preserves age, recovers PER to 25.34%
+ details
Freezing the first 6 transformer layers prevents CPT from overwriting the age-encoding early layers while still allowing task-specific adaptation in later layers. The result: PER recovers to 25.34% (within 0.15% of the pre-trained baseline) while preserving the developmental information that CPT would have destroyed.
6
Phoneme Recovery
Liquids 99% recovery, stops 127%; category-level validation
+ details
Phoneme-level analysis shows that Freeze-6 selectively recovers the categories most damaged by CPT. Liquids (the most damaged, +16.74% PER increase under CPT) recover 99% of the way back to baseline. Stops over-recover to 127%. Late-acquired phonemes (age 7-8: /ɹ, ɾ/) recover 92%, confirming the freeze protects developmentally informative categories.
7
Cross-Model Validation
Age universal (4 models 75.5-78.1%), rate WavLM-specific
+ details
Testing across four SSL models (WavLM-Large, HuBERT-Large, wav2vec2-Large, WavLM-Base+) reveals that age encoding is universal: all models achieve 75.5-78.1% at Layer 1-2. In contrast, the late-layer rate hierarchy (81.4% at L24) is WavLM-specific. This separation between universal and model-specific developmental encodings has not been reported before.
